Mathematicians Who Died Before 40

Six brilliant minds, each gone too soon — explore their lives, their breakthroughs, and the mark they left on mathematics.

Niels Henrik Abel

1802–1829 (died at 26)

Contribution: Proved that a general algebraic solution to the quintic equation is impossible.

Died of tuberculosis in poverty, just two days before a letter arrived offering him a professorship.

Evariste Galois

1811–1832 (died at 20)

Contribution: Building on Abel's works, founded group theory, laying the groundwork for modern abstract algebra.

Wrote down his life's mathematical work in a single frantic night before dying in a duel the next morning.

Bernhard Riemann

1826–1866 (died at 39)

Contribution: Developed foundational ideas in geometry and analysis, including the famous Riemann Hypothesis.

His hypothesis about prime numbers remains one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics today.

Blaise Pascal

1623–1662 (died at 39)

Contribution: Pioneered probability theory and created Pascal's Triangle, plus one of the first mechanical calculators.

Built his calculating machine as a teenager to help his father with tax calculations.

Srinivasa Ramanujan

1887–1920 (died at 32)

Contribution: Self-taught genius who produced thousands of original results in number theory and infinite series.

Compiled nearly 3,900 results largely without formal training or rigorous proof, many of which were later confirmed correct.
