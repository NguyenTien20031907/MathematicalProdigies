Mathematical Prodigies

Brilliant minds, brief lives — the mathematicians who changed the world before turning 40.

A Short Time, A Lasting Legacy

Throughout history, some of mathematics' most groundbreaking ideas came from minds that burned brightly and briefly. This site explores six mathematicians who died before the age of 40 — yet whose discoveries still shape how we understand numbers, space, and the universe today. From a teenager who founded an entire branch of algebra to a self-taught genius who astonished the world with his intuition, their stories are a reminder that impact isn't measured in years.

Featured: Évariste Galois

Died at just 20 years old after a duel, Galois spent his final night frantically writing down the mathematical ideas that would later become the foundation of group theory — a framework now essential to modern algebra, cryptography, and physics.
