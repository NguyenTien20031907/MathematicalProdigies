Timeline of Short Lives

Six extraordinary minds, laid out across the centuries they lived in. See how young each one was when they changed mathematics forever.

  1. Jun 19, 1623

    Blaise Pascal

    Born in Clermont-Ferrand, France, into a family that encouraged his early fascination with mathematics.

  2. 1642

    Blaise Pascal

    At 19, builds the Pascaline, one of the first mechanical calculators, to help his father with tax calculations.

  3. Summer 1654

    Blaise Pascal

    Exchanges letters with Pierre de Fermat on a gambling problem, laying the foundations of modern probability theory.

  4. Aug 19, 1662

    Blaise Pascal

    Dies at 39 in Paris, his health broken by years of illness, having spent his final years writing on philosophy and faith.

  5. Aug 5, 1802

    Niels Henrik Abel

    Born in Nedstrand, Norway, one of seven children in a family that struggled financially for most of his life.

  6. 1824

    Niels Henrik Abel

    At 21, proves that no general algebraic formula can solve fifth-degree equations, settling a question mathematicians had chased for centuries.

  7. 1826

    Niels Henrik Abel

    Travels through Europe seeking recognition, and develops what becomes known as Abelian functions, though his work is largely overlooked.

  8. Apr 6, 1829

    Niels Henrik Abel

    Dies of tuberculosis at 26 in Norway, just two days before a letter arrives offering him a professorship in Berlin.

  9. Oct 25, 1811

    Évariste Galois

    Born in Bourg-la-Reine, near Paris, showing an early gift for mathematics that his formal schooling struggled to nurture.

  10. 1829

    Évariste Galois

    Submits his first major papers on the theory of equations to the French Academy of Sciences; the manuscript is lost by a reviewer.

  11. 1830–31

    Évariste Galois

    Becomes swept up in the political upheaval following the July Revolution and is twice imprisoned for his radical activities.

  12. May 31, 1832

    Évariste Galois

    Dies at 20 from wounds suffered in a duel the previous morning, after spending his final night frantically writing down the ideas that would found group theory.

  13. Dec 10, 1815

    Ada Lovelace

    Born in London, daughter of poet Lord Byron, and raised with a rigorous focus on mathematics and logic.

  14. 1833

    Ada Lovelace

    At 17, meets inventor Charles Babbage and becomes fascinated by his proposed Analytical Engine, a mechanical general-purpose computer.

  15. 1843

    Ada Lovelace

    Publishes translated notes on the Analytical Engine containing an algorithm to compute Bernoulli numbers — now recognized as the first published computer program.

  16. Nov 27, 1852

    Ada Lovelace

    Dies of cancer at 36 in London, over a century before her vision of computing would become reality.

  17. Sep 17, 1826

    Bernhard Riemann

    Born in Breselenz, Germany, the son of a poor Lutheran pastor, and initially trained for theology before turning to mathematics.

  18. 1854

    Bernhard Riemann

    Delivers his habilitation lecture on the foundations of geometry, work later essential to Einstein's general theory of relativity.

  19. 1859

    Bernhard Riemann

    Publishes his paper on prime number distribution, introducing the Riemann Hypothesis — still unsolved more than 150 years later.

  20. Jul 20, 1866

    Bernhard Riemann

    Dies of tuberculosis at 39 while traveling in Selasca, Italy, seeking relief for his declining health.

  21. Dec 22, 1887

    Srinivasa Ramanujan

    Born in Erode, India, and begins teaching himself advanced mathematics from a single outdated textbook.

  22. Jan 16, 1913

    Srinivasa Ramanujan

    Sends a letter packed with original theorems to Cambridge mathematician G. H. Hardy, who is stunned by their brilliance.

  23. 1918

    Srinivasa Ramanujan

    Becomes one of the youngest Fellows ever elected to the Royal Society, honored for his contributions to number theory.

  24. Apr 26, 1920

    Srinivasa Ramanujan

    Dies at 32 shortly after returning to India, his health broken by illness and the harsh wartime conditions in England.
